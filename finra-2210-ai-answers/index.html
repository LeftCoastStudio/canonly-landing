Canonly/FINRA 2210 and the SEC Marketing Rule in AI answers

Wealth and advisory brands appear in AI answers compliantly by composing from principal-approved content blocks that carry their own disclosures.

Wealth and advisory firms show up in AI answers compliantly when FINRA Rule 2210 and SEC Marketing Rule requirements are attached to content blocks in advance: a registered principal approves each block once, disclosures and fair-and-balanced language travel with it, and marketing composes answer pages from approved blocks with a complete record of who approved what.

By Jason Garrett, founder of Canonly and Left Coast Agency / Coast Studio · Updated October 16, 2026

Why are wealth and advisory brands invisible in AI answers?

Financial-services buyers already use AI answers, and the firms are mostly absent from them. The Financial Brand reported in July 2026, citing the 5W Banking AI Visibility Index (31,500 prompts, five engines, 75 institutions), that 22 of the top 75 US banks hold under 0.3% of consumer-banking citations while Wikipedia, Bankrate and Investopedia supply roughly half. Adobe Analytics measured AI referral traffic to banking sites growing twelvefold between July 2024 and February 2025, with AI-referred visitors spending 45% more time per visit. Conductor's January 2026 survey found 94% of enterprises planning to increase AEO/GEO investment this year.

Advisory firms face a sharper version of the constraint. Every retail communication needs principal approval under FINRA Rule 2210, every advertisement must clear the SEC Marketing Rule's general prohibitions, and the content that engines cite (specific fees, specific strategies, specific outcomes) is exactly the content compliance scrutinizes hardest. The answer is to attach the rules to the content in advance.

What does FINRA Rule 2210 require?

Rule 2210 governs communications with the public by member firms. The provisions that shape answer content:

  • Classification. A retail communication is any written communication distributed to more than 25 retail investors in a 30-day period. A public web page is a retail communication.
  • Principal approval. A registered principal approves each retail communication before first use, subject to limited exceptions.
  • Content standards (2210(d)). Communications must be fair and balanced, must provide a sound basis for evaluating the facts, may not omit material facts, may not contain false, exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory or misleading statements, and may not predict or project performance except in narrow cases.
  • Filing. Certain retail communications are filed with FINRA's Advertising Regulation Department, including a new member's communications during its first year and specific product categories.
  • Recordkeeping. Communications and their approval records are retained under the rule and SEA Rule 17a-4, including the name of the approving principal and the date.

FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 (June 27, 2024) made the point that matters for AI drafting: the content standards under Rule 2210 apply whether a communication is generated by a person or by a technology tool.

What does the SEC Marketing Rule require?

Rule 206(4)-1 under the Advisers Act, with a compliance date of November 4, 2022, applies seven general prohibitions to every advertisement. An ad may not include an untrue statement of material fact or a misleading omission; may not make a material claim the adviser cannot substantiate on demand; may not create a misleading implication; may not discuss potential benefits without fair and balanced treatment of material risks and limitations; may not reference specific investment advice outside a fair and balanced presentation; may not present performance or time periods in an unbalanced way; and may not otherwise be materially misleading.

Testimonials and endorsements are permitted with clear and prominent disclosure of whether the promoter is a client and whether the promoter is compensated, a written agreement above de minimis compensation, adviser oversight, and bad-actor disqualification. Gross performance may appear only alongside net performance. Hypothetical performance requires policies ensuring relevance to the intended audience.

How does a governed block carry its disclosures?

A governed block is a reusable answer to one investor question with its compliance requirements attached as rules. A registered principal approves the block once. The disclosures travel with it. Composition rules enforce the pairings:

Claim typeRule enforced at composition
Performance figureRenders only with net performance and the required time periods; prohibited in retail blocks where projection would be implied
Testimonial or endorsementRenders only with client status and compensation disclosure; promoter must be on the approved-promoter list
Fee or cost statementRenders with the Form ADV reference and the "fees vary" qualifier approved for that context
Superlative or comparisonBlocked unless a substantiation record is linked
Any expired claimRefuses to render; pages using it are flagged

Marketing composes answer pages from approved blocks and ships them. Only new claims, new contexts and expired substantiation route back to the principal. The queue shrinks to the work that needed a principal in the first place.

How do you keep the record regulators expect?

Four properties make an audit trail usable in an examination. It is append-only, so events are written once and never edited. It is complete: which principal approved which block, on what substantiation, on what date, and which pages used it. It is versioned, so the firm can reconstruct what was approved and live on any past date. It is exportable as a per-page evidence packet in minutes. Because the record is generated as pages are composed, the 2210 approval log and the Marketing Rule substantiation file become by-products of publishing.

What does an AI-citable answer look like for an RIA?

Question: "What does a fee-only fiduciary advisor cost?" First sentence: the firm's approved fee statement, stated as a range with the basis for the range. Supporting claims: what the fee includes, how it is billed, and how it compares with the firm's own prior fee schedule (never a named competitor without substantiation). Required pairings: the Form ADV Part 2A reference and the approved fee disclosure. Structured-data twin: FAQPage JSON-LD generated from the block, disclosure included.

Frequently asked questions

Is a public web page a retail communication under FINRA Rule 2210?

Yes. A retail communication is any written communication made available to more than 25 retail investors within a 30-day period, which includes a public website, and it generally requires approval by a registered principal before first use.

Does FINRA treat AI-generated marketing content differently?

No. FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 (June 27, 2024) states that the content standards under Rule 2210 apply whether a communication is generated by a person or by a technology tool.

Can an RIA use client testimonials in AI-citable content?

Yes, under the SEC Marketing Rule, with clear and prominent disclosure of whether the promoter is a client and whether the promoter is compensated, a written agreement above de minimis compensation, adviser oversight, and bad-actor disqualification.

Can performance figures appear in an answer block?

Only when the block also presents net performance for the required time periods, and when hypothetical performance, if used, is covered by policies ensuring it is relevant to the intended audience. Composition rules enforce these pairings automatically.

What record does a firm need for each published page?

The approving principal, the approval date, the claims and substantiation used, and the version of the page that was live on any given date. An append-only, versioned, exportable log satisfies FINRA recordkeeping and Marketing Rule substantiation requests.
