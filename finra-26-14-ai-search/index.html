Canonly/FINRA Notice 26-14 and AI search

FINRA's risk-based review proposal would let firms publish approved answer content faster, if they can show which language is already approved.

Notice 26-14 proposes that broker-dealers stop requiring principal approval of every retail communication and instead decide, through written risk-based procedures, which categories need pre-use approval. If adopted, a firm could treat answer content assembled from already-approved language as a lower-risk category and publish the specific, current answers AI engines cite. The proposal is not in effect today.

By Jason Garrett, founder of Canonly and Left Coast Agency / Coast Studio · Updated October 28, 2026

What did FINRA propose?

FINRA published Regulatory Notice 26-14 on July 9, 2026, requesting comment on amendments to Rule 2210, Communications with the Public. Comments closed September 11, 2026. Any final amendments would require a separate SEC rule filing and approval, so current requirements still apply.

AreaCurrent Rule 2210Proposed in Notice 26-14
Pre-use approvalA qualified principal approves each retail communication before use.Written procedures decide which categories need principal pre-use approval, using eight nonexclusive risk factors.
Content without pre-use approvalNot applicable.Requires training, documentation, ongoing surveillance, follow-up and evidence of implementation available to FINRA on request.
Social mediaStatic and interactive content are supervised differently.The distinction is removed, and one risk-based standard applies.
AI-generated contentThe firm is responsible.Unchanged. Rules are technology-neutral; AI tools may be part of a supervisory system if vetted, tested and monitored.
Retail research reportsPre-use approval.Pre-use approval retained.

The risk factors FINRA lists include product complexity, the preparer's experience, whether the content recommends or promotes a product, whether it targets a specific audience, whether it includes performance data, rankings or comparisons, the medium and distribution method, and the history of concerns found in prior reviews.

Why does this matter for AI search?

Answer engines cite content that is specific, structured and current. Under blanket pre-approval, every new answer page waits in a principal's queue, so firms publish little and third-party sites answer investors' questions instead. A risk-based model would give a firm a documented reason to move certain content faster. The content standards stay the same: every communication must be fair and balanced and free of promissory or misleading statements.

Where is the defensible line?

The cleanest low-risk category is content built only from language a principal has already approved, with its required disclosures attached. Contract teams have run this model with clause libraries for years, and it is how Canonly works:

  • Claim and block library. A principal approves each claim, phrasing and disclosure once, with an expiry date.
  • Governed composition. Marketing assembles answer pages from approved blocks, required disclosures attach automatically, and expired language refuses to render.
  • Exception-only review. New language routes to a principal, which concentrates review on the factors Notice 26-14 treats as higher risk.
  • Evidence. An append-only audit trail shows what was approved, by whom and where it was published, which is the implementation evidence the proposal asks firms to keep.

What can firms do before any rule changes?

  1. Inventory retail communications against the eight risk factors.
  2. List the educational questions investors ask AI tools, and match each to approved language that already answers it.
  3. Document approved claims and disclosures as reusable blocks with owners and expiry dates.
  4. Measure a baseline of how often AI answers cite the firm.

Frequently asked questions

What is FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-14?

Regulatory Notice 26-14, published July 9, 2026, requested comment on amendments to FINRA Rule 2210. The central proposal replaces the requirement that a principal approve each retail communication before use with written, risk-based procedures in which each firm decides which categories need pre-use approval. Comments closed September 11, 2026. The proposal is not in effect and would require an SEC rule filing.

Does Notice 26-14 create a separate rule for AI-generated communications?

No. FINRA restates that its rules are technology-neutral and that a firm is responsible for a communication whether a person or an AI tool produced it. FINRA says generative AI tools may be part of a reasonably designed supervisory system if they are vetted, tested and monitored.

How would risk-based review affect content AI answer engines cite?

Firms could place lower-risk categories, such as educational answers assembled only from previously approved language with their disclosures attached, outside per-piece pre-use approval, provided they keep training, surveillance and evidence of implementation. That would let firms publish answer content at the pace answer engines reward.

Can a firm rely on Notice 26-14 today?

No. Notice 26-14 is a proposal. Current Rule 2210 pre-use approval requirements apply until amendments are approved and effective. Firms can prepare by inventorying communications by risk category and documenting which language is already approved.
