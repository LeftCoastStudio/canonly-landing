Canonly/MLR review and AI search

Pharma brands earn AI citations inside MLR review by approving claims once and pairing every efficacy statement with its safety information automatically.

Pharma marketing gets cited in AI answers without breaking OPDP rules when MLR approves a claim library once, each claim carries its substantiation, required Important Safety Information and expiry, and answer pages are composed only from in-date approved claims. Review then applies to new claims, and every published page keeps a complete audit trail.

By Jason Garrett, founder of Canonly and Left Coast Agency / Coast Studio · Updated October 16, 2026

Why does MLR review block AI visibility?

Medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review was built for launches, brochures and quarterly campaigns. It reviews each asset from scratch, usually in three or more rounds at one to three weeks per round. AI answer engines reward the opposite pattern: dozens of question-shaped pages per product, each refreshed when the facts change. Forty answer pages for one brand, pushed through asset-by-asset review, consume roughly a year of review capacity before the first update ships.

The enforcement climate makes shortcuts unthinkable. On September 9, 2025, FDA announced it would issue approximately 100 cease-and-desist letters over misleading direct-to-consumer drug advertising, and within a week it had posted 40 untitled letters and 35 warning letters, all dated September 9 (FDA Law Blog, September 2025). Meanwhile the 5W AI Visibility Index for Q2 2026 found roughly a quarter of all pharma AI citation share concentrated in two companies. Brands cannot review faster and cannot review less. They can change what gets reviewed.

What does OPDP require of promotional content?

FDA's Office of Prescription Drug Promotion (OPDP) applies the same standards to a web FAQ or an AI-citable answer page as to a print ad. The requirements that matter most for answer content are these:

  • Consistency with approved labeling. Claims stay inside the approved indication and the prescribing information. Off-label suggestions in promotional content draw letters.
  • Fair balance. Under 21 CFR 202.1(e), risk information must be presented with prominence and readability comparable to the efficacy claims. In practice this means the Important Safety Information (ISI) travels with every benefit statement.
  • Substantiation. Efficacy and safety claims rest on substantial evidence, generally adequate and well-controlled studies, and the support must be on file.
  • Submission at first use. Promotional labeling and advertisements are submitted to OPDP on Form FDA 2253 at the time of initial dissemination.
  • No omission of material facts. A technically true claim that leaves out a limitation or a contraindication is still misleading.

None of these rules prevents a brand from publishing answer pages. They set the conditions every page must satisfy, and those conditions can be encoded once.

How does a pre-approved claim library work for pharma?

Every claim a brand is allowed to make already exists somewhere MLR approved it: in the prescribing information, in the last cleared brochure, in the current ISI. The first build of a claim library is an archaeology project. A working session of one to two days per brand extracts each distinct claim from recent approved assets and records the metadata that makes it safely reusable:

FieldPharma example
Claim in approved wording, plus approved variantsThe efficacy statement as it appears in the cleared asset, with the short form approved for banners
SubstantiationLink to the PI section or the pivotal-trial reference on file
Approved contexts and required pairingsBranded consumer pages; must render with the ISI block and boxed warning if applicable
Prohibited contextsUnbranded disease-awareness pages; comparisons to a named competitor; any audience outside the indication
Expiry and ownerRe-review on any label update or new safety communication; named MLR owner

MLR ratifies the library once. From then on, marketing composes pages from approved claims, and only claims outside the library return for review.

How do answer blocks pair efficacy claims with safety information?

An answer block states one patient or clinician question verbatim, answers it in the first sentence from an approved claim, and carries its pairings as enforced rules. A composition rule says: an efficacy claim renders with its fair-balance language or it does not render. The ISI block is attached to the claim, so a composer cannot build a page that shows the benefit without the risk. The same rule applies to the structured-data twin: the FAQPage JSON-LD answer text is generated from the block, safety sentence included, so an engine that quotes the answer quotes the balance with it. Markup and visible copy come from one source and cannot drift apart, which removes a compliance failure mode as well as an SEO one.

Example. Question: "Who should not take [brand]?" First sentence: the approved contraindication statement. Supporting claims: the approved warnings summary. Required pairing: the full ISI block and a link to the prescribing information. The block ships to the brand FAQ page, the patient support site and the HCP portal, and every copy inherits the next label update.

How are substantiation and expiry handled?

A claim library is only as good as its currency. Each claim links to its source of record and carries an expiry trigger: a label update, a new safety communication, a superseded study or a fixed review date. An expired claim is pulled from circulation automatically, and every page that used it is flagged for recomposition. A quarterly claim review re-substantiates aging claims and retires obsolete ones. Versioning preserves what was approved and live on any past date, because OPDP inquiries are about a date in the past.

What still goes to MLR review?

Exception-only review keeps the reviewers in control of everything novel. Still routed to MLR: a claim with no prior approval, an approved claim used in a materially new context or channel, comparative or superiority language, and any claim whose substantiation has expired. Shipping without a review round: any page or answer block composed entirely of in-date approved claims in approved contexts. When OPDP asks who approved a statement and on what basis, the evidence packet (content, claims, substantiation, approval events) is a two-minute export.

Frequently asked questions

Can pharma brands publish AI-optimized content without OPDP risk?

Yes, when every claim on the page is drawn from an MLR-approved claim library, each efficacy claim renders with its fair-balance language, the content stays within the approved indication, and promotional pieces are submitted on Form FDA 2253 at first dissemination.

What is ISI pairing?

ISI pairing is a composition rule that attaches the Important Safety Information to every efficacy or benefit claim so the two cannot be separated. The rule applies to the visible page and to the FAQPage JSON-LD generated from the same block.

How does a claim library handle label updates?

Each claim carries an expiry trigger tied to its source of record. A label update or new safety communication expires the affected claims, removes them from circulation automatically, and flags every page that used them for recomposition.

What does MLR still review under this model?

New claims, approved claims used in a materially new context or channel, comparative or superiority language, and any claim whose substantiation has expired. Pages composed entirely from in-date approved claims ship without a review round.

Does AI drafting fit inside MLR review?

It does when the AI drafts inside the claim library, constrained to approved claims and enforced pairings. The output is compliant by construction, and anything the draft introduces outside the library routes to MLR as an exception.
