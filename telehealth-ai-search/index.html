Canonly/Telehealth and AI search

Telehealth brands earn AI citations by publishing approved answers to the questions patients ask before they choose a provider.

Patients now ask AI tools which telehealth provider to use, what it costs and whether it is safe. Answer engines cite whoever answers those questions directly, and prescription brands usually lose that slot to review sites and affiliates because every sentence needs clinical and legal review. The fix is to approve claims and content blocks once, pair each with its required safety language, and compose answer pages only from approved blocks.

By Jason Garrett, founder of Canonly and Left Coast Agency / Coast Studio · Updated October 28, 2026

What do patients ask AI before they choose a provider?

For GLP-1, hormone therapy, dermatology, mental health and sexual health providers, the recurring questions are cost, insurance coverage, eligibility, state availability, what the visit involves, how a clinician decides whether to prescribe, side effects, and whether the company is legitimate. The brand already knows each answer and has usually approved it somewhere: an FAQ, a consent flow, a support macro or an ad.

Why do telehealth brands lose the citation?

The approved language exists, but it is scattered across ads, intake flows and help centers, and it rarely appears on the site as a plain, current answer. Third-party pages fill the gap. Those pages may quote outdated pricing, imply guaranteed outcomes, or describe compounded and branded medication interchangeably, which creates risk the brand never approved.

How does the review-once model work for telehealth?

  • Claim library. Each claim carries its substantiation, approved phrasings, owner and expiry date.
  • Safety pairing. Efficacy statements render only with their required safety language attached.
  • Answer blocks. Short, structured answers to the questions above, each with a clinical reviewer and review date.
  • Exception-only review. New language routes to medical and legal review; approved language publishes without a queue.

This is the operating model Canonly applies to pharma MLR review, adapted for telehealth teams that move faster and review with fewer people. The pharma version is described in MLR review and AI search.

Frequently asked questions

Why don't AI answers cite telehealth brands?

Prescription telehealth brands publish cautiously because every efficacy, pricing and eligibility statement needs medical and legal review. Answer engines then cite review sites, publishers and affiliate comparison pages, which often describe the brand inaccurately.

What content do answer engines cite for telehealth questions?

They favor direct answers to specific questions: cost, insurance coverage, eligibility, state availability, what the visit involves, how prescribing decisions are made, and side effects. Pages that state these plainly, with a named clinical reviewer and a current review date, are easier to cite.

How can a telehealth brand publish faster without adding compliance risk?

Approve claims and content blocks once, each with its substantiation, required safety language and expiry date, then compose answer pages only from approved blocks. New language goes to review, and approved language publishes without a queue.

Does this apply to compounded medications?

Yes, and the stakes are higher. A claim library keeps compounded and FDA-approved products described separately and accurately, which prevents the most common error third-party pages make about telehealth brands.
